# How I Run My AI Helpers

I'm not a programmer. I have about 20 AI helper bots that handle the boring parts of my work. This page explains how I keep them organized and how I stay in charge of them. You don't need to know any code to follow along.

## What my helpers do

Each helper has one job, like a small team where everyone knows their role:

- **The mail sorter** reads incoming email and decides which helper should deal with each message.
- **My assistant** is the only one that comes to me with questions, and it saves them up so I get one tidy list instead of a hundred interruptions.
- **Website helpers** each look after one website and keep it up to date.
- **Social media helpers** write draft posts for each platform.
- **The idea helper** looks at opportunities that come in and tells me which ones are worth my time.
- **The bookkeeping helper** collects receipts so tax time is easier.

## One shared to-do list

All my helpers use one shared to-do list in Notion (a free note-taking app). Every job gets one line on that list, with:

- who is doing it
- what happens next
- where the request came from

When a new request comes in, a helper checks the list first. If the job is already there, it updates that line instead of starting a new one, so nothing gets done twice.

When one helper finishes its part, it hands the job to the next helper by changing the name on that line and leaving a short note.

## My rule: nothing goes out without my yes

- Helpers can write drafts and get things ready. They never send an email, publish a post, sign up for anything or spend money until I say yes.
- Saying "that draft looks good" is not the same as saying "send it." I have to clearly say go.
- When they need a decision, they give me everything in one place: what it is, what will change, what it costs and when it happens.

## A job isn't finished until there's proof

A helper can't just say "done." It has to show me something real, like a link to the live post or a saved draft, before it crosses the job off.

## Instruction sheets

The `instruction-sheets` folder has the simple step-by-step instructions I give my helpers. You can copy them for your own helpers:

- **using-the-shared-to-do-list.md**: how to add, update and finish jobs on the list
- **handling-a-new-request.md**: what to do when a new email or request comes in
- **asking-me-for-a-yes.md**: how to ask me to approve something
- **double-checking-the-work.md**: how to prove something actually happened
- **crossing-a-job-off.md**: what to do when a job is truly finished
